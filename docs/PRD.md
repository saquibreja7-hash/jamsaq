## 2. Purpose

This PRD defines the market opportunity, positioning, target customers, services, website requirements, lead-generation funnel, and operating roadmap for Jamsaq as a web and mobile app design agency.

Jamsaq should not compete as a commodity "cheap website" vendor. The agency should position itself as a practical design and build partner for founders, service businesses, local brands, and early-stage teams that need credible web presence, mobile-first UX, and clear conversion paths.

## 3. Market Research Summary

### 3.1 Web design demand

- IBISWorld estimates the U.S. web design services market at **$47.4B in 2025**, with **1.5% growth in 2025** and a **2.3% CAGR from 2020 to 2025**.
- Clutch reports that **83% of small businesses have a website in 2025**, up from **65% in 2018**, which means the market is mature but still leaves a meaningful offline or underdeveloped segment.
- Clutch also frames websites as a lead-generation and brand-control channel. Businesses without a website rely more on referrals, marketplaces, and social channels, but lose control of credibility, search visibility, and conversion paths.
- The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics projects **7% employment growth for web developers and digital designers from 2024 to 2034**, faster than the average for all occupations. BLS also notes that e-commerce growth and mobile device usage continue to drive demand for mobile-ready sites and interfaces.

### 3.2 Mobile app and mobile UX demand

- IMARC estimates the U.S. mobile app development market reached **$60.39B in 2025** and projects **$150.22B by 2034**, a **10.66% CAGR from 2026 to 2034**.
- Sensor Tower reports that mobile users spent **4.2 trillion hours** in apps and consumer app spend reached **$150B** in its State of Mobile 2025 report.
- GSMA reports that **4.7B people**, or **58% of the world's population**, used mobile internet by the end of 2024, with mobile internet users projected to reach **5.5B by 2030**.
- GoodFirms' 2025 app development survey found that security, privacy, AI-assisted development, cross-platform apps, voice interfaces, adaptive UX, and minimalist design are major buyer and builder concerns.

### 3.3 Pricing and buyer expectations

- Clutch's 2026 web design pricing guide places typical web design company rates around **$100-$149/hour**.
- Clutch's 2026 mobile app pricing guide says most app development projects reviewed on Clutch fall between **$10,000-$49,999**, while the average app development project cost is about **$90,780** with an average timeline around **11 months**.
- The market therefore has two clear buyer groups:
  - Budget-conscious buyers who want a practical, conversion-ready website without enterprise overhead.
  - Product-minded buyers who need app UX, prototype validation, MVP design, or a cross-platform product build.

## 4. Market Implications for Jamsaq

Jamsaq should occupy the "boutique product studio for practical launches" lane.

The winning position:

- More strategic than freelancers and template shops.
- Faster and more accessible than large agencies.
- More accountable than AI-only or no-code-only builders.
- More design-led than general software contractors.

Core market gap:

Small teams often know they need a better website or app, but they do not know how to turn business goals into scope, UX, content, design, and launch. Jamsaq should sell clarity first, then design and build.

## 5. Target Customers

### 5.1 Primary persona: Founder launching a service or product

- Company stage: Idea to early revenue
- Team size: 1-20
- Budget: $4,000-$20,000 for website or MVP work
- Needs:
  - Credible web presence
  - Clear offer and positioning
  - Landing page or small marketing site
  - Mobile-first design
  - Pitch-ready product prototype
- Pain points:
  - Does not know what to build first
  - Has weak branding or inconsistent messaging
  - Needs speed without looking cheap
  - Wants a partner who can explain tradeoffs clearly

### 5.2 Secondary persona: Local or service business owner

- Company stage: Existing business with outdated site
- Team size: 1-50
- Budget: $3,000-$12,000
- Needs:
  - Better search and local credibility
  - Mobile-friendly site
  - Contact, booking, quote, or lead forms
  - Portfolio, testimonials, and service pages
- Pain points:
  - Existing site is slow, dated, or hard to update
  - Leads come mostly from referrals or social media
  - Owner lacks time to manage technical details

### 5.3 Tertiary persona: Startup product team

- Company stage: Pre-seed to seed
- Team size: 3-30
- Budget: $10,000-$50,000+
- Needs:
  - UX research and product flows
  - Figma prototype
  - Design system
  - MVP web app or mobile app design
  - Investor/demo-ready experience
- Pain points:
  - Engineers need clearer product requirements
  - Founders need customer validation
  - Existing product feels inconsistent or hard to use

## 8. Website Product Requirements

The Jamsaq website is the first product surface for the agency. It must convert skeptical buyers into qualified conversations.

### 8.1 Primary goals

- Explain what Jamsaq does in under 5 seconds.
- Build trust with evidence, process, and clear pricing anchors.
- Capture qualified leads through a low-friction project brief.
- Support future case studies, service pages, and content marketing.

### 8.2 Core pages for MVP

1. Homepage
   - Hero with clear positioning
   - Primary CTA: "Start a project"
   - Secondary CTA: "View services"
   - Service overview
   - Selected work or sample concepts
   - Process
   - Pricing anchors
   - Contact form

2. Services
   - Web Launch
   - Web Growth
   - Mobile App UX Sprint
   - MVP Product Design and Build
   - Retainer
   - Who each service is for
   - Deliverables and timelines

3. Work
   - Case studies once available
   - Until then, use concept studies or internal demo projects with clear labels

4. About
   - Agency mission
   - Founder/team story
   - Working principles
   - Tools and process

5. Contact
   - Project brief form
   - Budget range
   - Timeline
   - Project type
   - Business goals

### 8.3 Functional requirements

- Responsive layout for mobile, tablet, and desktop.
- Accessible semantic HTML with visible focus states.
- Contact form with validation.
- Email routing or CRM capture for leads.
- Analytics events for CTA clicks, form starts, form submissions, and pricing clicks.
- Basic SEO metadata per page.
- Open Graph image and social preview metadata.
- Performance target: Lighthouse Performance 90+ on production.
- Accessibility target: Lighthouse Accessibility 95+.
- Form spam protection before public launch.

### 8.4 Content requirements

- Avoid generic agency claims such as "we create beautiful websites."
- Use buyer language:
  - "launch a credible site"
  - "turn an app idea into a testable prototype"
  - "fix a website that is not converting"
  - "make the product easier to understand"
- Include pricing anchors, not hidden pricing.
- Include process detail so buyers know what happens after inquiry.
- Include outcome language around trust, leads, clarity, speed, and usability.

### 8.5 Technical stack

The Jamsaq website should use a stack that supports performance, SEO, maintainability, and future service-page expansion.

- Framework: Next.js App Router
- Language: TypeScript
- Styling: Tailwind CSS
- Icons: lucide-react
- Hosting target: Vercel or another Next.js-compatible platform
- Initial rendering model: static-first marketing pages, with dynamic/server functionality added only for forms, analytics, CRM, or CMS needs

Stack requirements:

- Keep public marketing pages fast and crawlable.
- Prefer server components unless client-side state or browser APIs are required.
- Use Tailwind utilities for layout and component styling.
- Keep global CSS limited to Tailwind imports, theme variables, resets, and base accessibility styles.
- Avoid adding a CMS, analytics provider, or form backend until the relevant task is selected.

## 9. Lead Funnel Requirements

### 9.1 Lead qualification fields

- Name
- Email
- Company or project name
- Project type
  - Website
  - Website redesign
  - Mobile app UX
  - MVP build
  - Ongoing support
- Budget range
- Desired launch timeline
- Short project description
- Current website or app URL, optional

### 9.2 Lead scoring

High-fit leads:

- Budget is $4,000+
- Clear business goal
- Timeline is within 1-3 months
- Founder, owner, or decision maker
- Needs strategy plus execution

Low-fit leads:

- Wants a full custom app for under $3,000
- No clear goal or owner
- Requires unsupported regulated workflows without budget
- Wants only commodity labor

### 9.3 Post-submit experience

- Show confirmation immediately.
- Send automated email with:
  - Thanks
  - Expected response time
  - Optional scheduling link
  - What to prepare for discovery call
- Internally notify Jamsaq with lead score and project summary.

## 10. Mobile App Design Agency Requirements

Jamsaq should support mobile app design without overpromising full enterprise app development on day one.

### 10.1 MVP app-design deliverables

- Product brief
- User personas
- User flows
- Information architecture
- Low-fidelity wireframes
- High-fidelity Figma screens
- Clickable prototype
- Design system starter
- Edge cases and empty states
- Developer handoff notes

### 10.2 Mobile UX quality bar

- Thumb-friendly navigation and tap targets.
- Loading, empty, error, success, and permission states.
- Authentication and onboarding flows where relevant.
- Accessibility contrast and scalable type.
- Privacy-first data collection.
- Security and trust patterns for accounts, payments, and sensitive data.

### 10.3 App categories to target first

- Founder MVPs
- Booking and scheduling apps
- Service marketplaces
- Local business customer portals
- Lightweight internal operations tools
- AI-assisted workflow tools

Avoid initially:

- Complex fintech or healthcare apps requiring heavy compliance.
- Gaming apps.
- Hardware-dependent apps.
- Large enterprise transformations without partner capacity.

## 11. Competitive Landscape

### 11.1 Competitor categories

1. DIY builders
   - Examples: Wix, Squarespace, Webflow templates
   - Strength: low cost, quick setup
   - Weakness: weak strategy, generic output, buyer must do the thinking

2. Freelancers
   - Strength: affordable, flexible
   - Weakness: inconsistent process, limited capacity, variable reliability

3. Boutique agencies
   - Strength: strategy, design quality, accountability
   - Weakness: higher cost, limited availability

4. Development shops
   - Strength: engineering depth
   - Weakness: often weaker brand, UX, copy, and conversion thinking

5. AI/no-code product builders
   - Strength: speed and lower prototype cost
   - Weakness: still needs judgment, QA, differentiation, and maintainability

### 11.2 Jamsaq competitive angle

Jamsaq should blend the speed of modern tooling with the judgment of a design partner:

- Strategy-led discovery
- Mobile-first UX
- Conversion-ready design
- Clear scope and pricing
- Build-ready implementation
- Post-launch iteration

## 15. Risks and Mitigations

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Market is crowded with low-cost designers | Price pressure | Lead with strategy, proof, process, and outcomes |
| AI builders lower perceived value | Buyers expect cheaper work | Position Jamsaq as judgment, QA, conversion, and maintainability |
| No case studies at launch | Lower trust | Use honest concept studies, audits, and before/after demos |
| App development scope can balloon | Margin risk | Sell app UX sprint before build; require PRD and fixed MVP scope |
| Lead quality is low | Wasted time | Use budget/timeline fields and lead scoring |
| Delivery capacity is limited | Missed timelines | Start with focused packages and partner for complex builds |
